Making a Valid Will in Lagos: The Formalities That Matter

A will that fails on a technicality leaves an estate to be distributed as if no will existed. The requirements are simple, but they must be followed.

October 20264 min readHeze Legal Advisory

A will lets you decide who receives your property, who manages your estate and who cares for your minor children. In Lagos State, wills are governed by the Wills Law of Lagos State. Its formal requirements are straightforward, but a mistake can invalidate the document.

Writing and signature

The will must be in writing and signed by the person making it, or by someone else in their presence and at their direction. The signature should appear at the end of the will.

Two witnesses

The signature must be made or acknowledged in the presence of two witnesses who are present at the same time. Each witness must then sign in the presence of the person making the will.

Do not let beneficiaries witness

A gift to a witness, or to a witness’s spouse, will generally fail, even though the rest of the will remains valid. Choose independent witnesses.

Appoint executors

Name executors you trust to administer the estate, and consider a substitute in case your first choice cannot act.

Keep it safe and current

A will can be deposited for safekeeping at the Probate Registry. Review it after major life events such as marriage, divorce, the birth of a child or the sale of a business.

Limits to know

Depending on your circumstances, customary or Islamic law may limit what can be left by will. Take advice so that the will achieves what you intend.
