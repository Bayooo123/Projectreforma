The Arbitration and Mediation Act 2023: Six Changes Commercial Parties Should Know

Nigeria’s arbitration statute now offers emergency relief, third-party funding and a statutory framework for mediation. Here is what it means for contracts in force and those still being negotiated.

October 20265 min readHeze Legal Advisory

In May 2023, Nigeria replaced the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, the statute that had governed arbitration since 1988, with the Arbitration and Mediation Act 2023. The new Act brings Nigerian law closer to international practice and gives parties more tools to resolve disputes quickly and to enforce the outcome.

1. Emergency arbitrators

A party that needs urgent interim relief can now apply for an emergency arbitrator before the full tribunal is constituted. This reduces the need to approach the courts for protective orders at the start of a dispute.

2. Third-party funding

The Act expressly recognises third-party funding of arbitration. Funding arrangements must be disclosed, but the old common law objections of maintenance and champerty no longer stand in the way.

3. Limitation periods

When computing the limitation period for enforcing an award, the time between the commencement of the arbitration and the date of the award is excluded. This addresses a long-standing risk that a party could succeed in an arbitration only to find enforcement time-barred.

4. An optional award review tribunal

Parties may agree that an award will be reviewed by an award review tribunal before any application is made to court to set it aside. The option applies only where the arbitration agreement provides for it.

5. Joinder and consolidation

The Act allows additional parties to be joined and related proceedings to be consolidated in defined circumstances, which helps in multi-party and multi-contract disputes.

6. Mediation on a statutory footing

For the first time, mediation has a statutory framework in Nigeria, together with a route to enforce settlement agreements reached through mediation.

What to do now

Businesses should review the dispute resolution clauses in their key contracts. Clauses drafted with the old Act in mind may not take advantage of emergency relief, award review or mediation. New contracts should state the seat, the rules, the number of arbitrators and whether the parties opt in to award review.
