Before You Sign: Five Clauses That Decide How Commercial Disputes End

Most disputes are won or lost on terms agreed long before anything went wrong. These are the clauses to read twice.

October 20264 min readHeze Legal Advisory

Commercial contracts are usually negotiated around price, scope and timelines. The clauses that matter most when a relationship breaks down often receive the least attention. Five deserve a careful read before any signature.

1. Dispute resolution

Decide whether disputes go to court, to arbitration, or to mediation first. If arbitration, name the seat, the rules, the number of arbitrators and the language. An unclear clause invites a dispute about the dispute.

2. Governing law and jurisdiction

State which law governs the contract and which courts have jurisdiction. In cross-border transactions, this choice affects everything from interpretation to enforcement.

3. Limitation of liability

Caps on liability and exclusions of indirect loss allocate risk between the parties. Make sure they reflect the commercial deal and are drafted clearly enough to be relied on.

4. Termination

Set out when each party can end the contract, what notice is required, and what happens to payments, materials and confidential information on termination.

5. Force majeure

Nigerian courts will not generally imply a force majeure clause. If you want relief for events beyond your control, the contract must provide for it, define the events covered, and set out the notice and mitigation steps.

A final check

Read the notices clause and confirm that the addresses are current. A valid claim can be undermined by a notice sent to the wrong place.
