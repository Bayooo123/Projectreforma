Shareholder Disputes in Private Companies: Options Before and During Litigation

When co-owners fall out, the company’s own documents and the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 offer more routes than a lawsuit.

October 20265 min readHeze Legal Advisory

Most private companies in Nigeria are owned by a small group of founders, family members or investors. When their relationship breaks down, the business can stall. The right response depends on what the company’s documents say and what the shareholders want to achieve.

Start with the documents

The articles of association and any shareholders’ agreement are the first point of reference. They may set out how deadlock is broken, how shares are valued and transferred, and how disputes must be resolved. Many agreements require negotiation, mediation or arbitration before any party goes to court.

Negotiated exits

A buy-out is often the most practical outcome. One shareholder acquires the other’s shares at an agreed or independently determined value, and the business continues. Early advice on valuation and tax helps the parties reach terms.

Statutory remedies

Where negotiation fails, the Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 gives shareholders remedies. A member may apply to court where the company’s affairs are conducted in a manner that is unfairly prejudicial to, or unfairly discriminatory against, a member. A shareholder may also, with the leave of court, bring a derivative action on the company’s behalf where directors have breached their duties and the company will not act. In serious cases, the court may order the company to be wound up on just and equitable grounds.

Protecting the business in the meantime

Interim orders can preserve assets, bank accounts and the status quo while the dispute is resolved. Directors should continue to act in the company’s best interests and keep proper records throughout.

Preventing the next dispute

A clear shareholders’ agreement, with deadlock, exit and dispute resolution provisions, costs far less than litigation. Companies without one should consider putting one in place.
