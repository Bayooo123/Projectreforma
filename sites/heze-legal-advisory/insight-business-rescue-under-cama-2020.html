Business Rescue under CAMA 2020: Administration and Company Voluntary Arrangements

Nigerian insolvency law now offers ways to save a struggling company, not only to wind it up. Directors and creditors should know how the procedures work.

October 20265 min readHeze Legal Advisory

Before 2020, a Nigerian company in financial difficulty had few options beyond receivership or liquidation. The Companies and Allied Matters Act 2020 introduced business rescue procedures drawn from international practice, giving directors, creditors and shareholders tools to preserve a viable business.

Company voluntary arrangements

A company voluntary arrangement allows a company to agree a compromise or scheme with its creditors under the supervision of an insolvency practitioner. Once approved by the required majorities, the arrangement binds the creditors who were entitled to vote on it, including those who voted against it.

Administration

Administration places the company under the control of an administrator whose objective is to rescue the company as a going concern or, if that is not possible, to achieve a better result for creditors than an immediate winding up. A moratorium protects the company from most legal proceedings and enforcement action while the administrator works.

Netting

The Act also recognises netting of qualified financial contracts, giving financial institutions greater certainty about their exposures when a counterparty becomes insolvent.

For directors

Directors of a company in distress should act early. Delay narrows the options and can expose directors to personal liability. Taking advice before cash runs out allows a rescue to be considered while it is still achievable.

For creditors

Creditors should understand how a moratorium affects their security and claims, take part in creditors’ meetings, and assess whether a proposed arrangement offers a better return than liquidation.
